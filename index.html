What I do

  1. 01

    I make complex systems observable

    Distributed platforms are only as debuggable as the view you have into them. I build diagnostic tooling that takes whatever identifier an engineer starts with and resolves the full picture around it — turning a multi-day investigation into a single query.

  2. 02

    I lead critical escalations

    When a mission-critical system degrades, the technical fix is half the work. The other half is holding one clear, honest line across customer, engineering and leadership while the picture is still forming.

  3. 03

    I fix the class of problem, not the instance

    Every incident should leave something behind — a playbook, a new signal in telemetry, or a change to how the work is done. The goal is that the same problem never has to be solved twice.

  4. 04

    I teach what I learn

    Onboarding material, mentoring and regular sessions on the skills that are hardest to pick up alone — tracing a request end to end, backend investigation, running an escalation well. Ten engineers who can do the work beats being the only one who can.

How I think

Craftsmanship over ceremony

The quality of the tooling sets the ceiling on the quality of the work.

Scope is a form of kindness

A clearly bounded problem gets solved. An unbounded one just gets older.

Hold the line on evidence

Ambiguous data produces ambiguous conclusions — and in healthcare that is not a neutral outcome.

Build for the person behind you

Every tool and every write-up is one less thing the next engineer has to work out alone.

Reliability isn't the absence of failure. It's what you've built to meet it.
